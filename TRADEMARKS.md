The repository's MIT License covers copyright in project-authored code,
documentation, and assets. It does not grant a right to claim that a modified
deployment is the official VerifyFirst or TokimiSpace service.

“VerifyFirst”, “Verify1st”, “TokimiSpace”, the VerifyFirst logo, and the
`verify1st.tw` and `tokimi.space` identifiers are project marks for purposes of
this policy. This policy does not claim rights in third-party names listed in
[THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## Uses that do not require advance permission

- truthfully stating that software is based on or compatible with VerifyFirst;
- linking to the official project or citing it in research, news, education, or
  reviews;
- using screenshots of an unmodified official service to explain its behavior;
- retaining factual copyright and attribution notices required by MIT; and
- using the name in a repository fork description when the fork is clearly
  identified as unofficial.

These uses must not imply certification, endorsement, partnership, regulatory
approval, or operation by TokimiSpace.

## Uses that require permission

- operating a modified or third-party service under the VerifyFirst name or
  official logo;
- using a confusingly similar name, domain, app icon, or visual identity;
- presenting a fork, credential, Evidence Packet, verifier, or compliance result
  as issued, audited, or guaranteed by VerifyFirst; or
- using the marks in advertising or merchandise in a way that suggests an
  official relationship.

Public forks and redistributed products should choose their own name and logo,
while retaining the attribution required by MIT. Security research may use the
marks factually, but must not impersonate the official service or collect user
secrets under its identity.

Brand questions can be opened as a public discussion that contains no sensitive
information. Security-sensitive impersonation reports should use the private
channel in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
